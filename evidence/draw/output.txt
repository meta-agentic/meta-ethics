N=10 eligible judges, f=1 friend; baseline P(friend) = 0.100

(a) eligibility manipulation AFTER the seed is known (attacker removes <= r judges)
    r   live-eligibility P(friend)   frozen-at-filing P(friend)
    0   0.098                        0.101
    1   0.200                        0.101
    2   0.289                        0.101
    3   0.383                        0.098
    -> with live eligibility, one removal doubles the odds and three quadruple them; frozen, removals do nothing.
       The eligible set and its order must be fixed at filing, before the beacon value exists.

(b) frozen set, but the drawn judge is removed after the draw (each removal is a recorded act)
    advance-in-seed-order: mean removals to reach a friend = 4.39
    redraw with a fresh beacon value: mean removals to reach a friend = 4.61
    -> either way the cost is ~ N/2 removals, each a recorded act (H1). Fresh redraw is not cheaper.

(c) filer files k copies and withdraws the unfriendly ones (each filing a recorded act)
    k= 1  P(friend among k draws) = 0.100
    k= 2  P(friend among k draws) = 0.190
    k= 5  P(friend among k draws) = 0.410
    k=10  P(friend among k draws) = 0.651
    k=20  P(friend among k draws) = 0.878
    -> multiplicity is a real lever; cost is per filing (instance capacity) unless the draw binds the matter.

(d) beacon bias of b bits (e.g. block withholding, last-revealer)
    b=0  P(friend) = 0.100
    b=1  P(friend) = 0.190
    b=2  P(friend) = 0.344
    b=3  P(friend) = 0.570
    b=4  P(friend) = 0.815
    -> 3 bits of bias ~ 57%%; an unbiasable beacon (threshold or VDF based) is a parent requirement.

(e) seed from a value the filer or operator can foresee or steer (timestamp, resubmittable head)
       1 candidate values  P(friend) = 0.100
      10 candidate values  P(friend) = 0.651
      60 candidate values  P(friend) = 0.998
     600 candidate values  P(friend) = 1.000
    -> a timestamp is not a beacon; a transparency-log head is grindable by whoever can append to it.

(f) modulo bias of seed mod N
      8-bit seed: max/min residue frequency = 1.0400
     16-bit seed: max/min residue frequency = 1.0002
    256-bit seed: bias <= N / 2^256, negligible
    -> the reference shape's small integer seeds are illustrative only; the parent should require the seed be a hash.

(g) C1 honesty: rank-and-modulo under a renaming of judges
    fixed seed: drawn j09; after swapping names j00<->j09 the drawn name is j09, i.e. the judge formerly called j00 (equivariant: False)
    over seeds: min/max frequency = 0.096 / 0.103 (uniform: each ~0.100)
    -> the draw's fairness claim is uniformity over the frozen set, not symmetry of the rule set;
       as an attested fact it leaves C1's rule-set symmetry untouched, and it must be claimed as uniformity.
