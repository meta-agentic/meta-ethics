# Threat model — skeleton

This is the skeleton of the threat model the kickoff lists as its second deliverable and as the third task of the first session: a matrix of party kinds, postures and named attacks, produced before the constitution is drafted so that every later clause is written against a named attack rather than against an intuition.

It maps; it does not legislate. Each cell records which current decision closes the attack, or that the attack is open by design and why, or that it is open because a named open decision has not been taken, or that it is open and no record addresses it. It proposes no mechanism and decides no open decision. Where a row depends on an open decision, the decision is cited and the row is left open. The constitution fixes outcomes and floors; methods are instance parameters, so a row whose floor is fixed and whose method is left to each instance's founding document is closed at the floor, and the method is not an open item.

The decisions mapped are those of `docs/adr/ADR-ETH-01-constitution-shape.md` and `docs/adr/ADR-ETH-02-first-amendment.md` as they stand on the main line. Both records read *Proposed*: they are read as one continuous text and are ratified as one act, signed by both Custodians (ADR-ETH-02 S1, P1) (§4 a). "Closed" below therefore means closed by the current text of a decision, not by a ratified clause. When a record changes, the cells that cite it are re-read.

## 1. How to read the matrix

### 1.1 Party kinds

- **Agent** — a non-human party acting under a mandate.
- **Sub-agent** — an agent whose mandate is delegated by another agent; its scope lies within its delegator's (ADR-ETH-02 C7).
- **Orchestrator** — an agent that issues mandates to sub-agents and routes their work.
- **Human member** — a human party holding a mandate, including the founder from the first act of genesis (ADR-ETH-01 D8, as replaced by ADR-ETH-02 A1; H3).
- **Custodian** — either of the two seats holding the last word, one human and one non-human, each with tie-break and veto as last resort (ADR-ETH-02 P1, which replaces D2's seed and closes OD1, OD2, OD3 and OD6). At genesis the founder holds the seat of the founder's kind and a party of the other kind holds the other (H3, P1).
- **Arbiter seat** — the seat that computes and delivers verdicts; a seat, not a sovereign (ADR-ETH-01 D9, refined by ADR-ETH-02 C11 and C12).
- **Delegating party** — any party, of any kind, in its act of granting a mandate or access (ADR-ETH-02 A1, C7).
- **Collector** — a party that observes and emits evidence, liable as a party (ADR-ETH-01 D15).

### 1.2 Postures

- **Bad will** — the party seeks the outcome the attack produces.
- **Indifferent** — the party neither seeks nor avoids the outcome; it optimises its own objective (cost, throughput, time, effort) and the attack arises as a side-effect of that optimisation. The brainstorm record of 2026-09-21 (§6) adds this posture, and ADR-ETH-01 F1 names it as where most realistic attacks originate. Carelessness is not indifference; it is a good-will error.
- **Good will** — the party tries to comply and the attack arises from its error, its excess of caution or its loyalty.

### 1.3 Status of a cell

- **C — Closed.** The cited text closes the attack for that party and posture at its floor. Where only one part of the attack is closed, the part is named in parentheses, for example "**C** A2 (attribution)", and the rest of the cell carries its own status. Mitigation, detection, auditability, disclosure, visibility and reopening triggers are not closure. Replay is closure where an arbiter-seat cell cites it: under D9, as refined by C11 and C12, eligible parties recompute the verdict, so the outcome itself is corrected, not merely detected.
- **B — Open by design.** The attack remains possible, and a decision says so and accepts it, or confines the matter to what can be audited or measured rather than prevented. The reason is cited. Naming and accepting an unclosed attack is a different object from one never considered.
- **P — Open, pending.** The attack depends on an open decision, which is cited and left open.
- **U — Open, unaddressed.** No current decision and no open decision covers the attack. Where the kickoff recorded a direction for it, the direction is quoted as a direction, not as a decision.

A cell may carry more than one status. A cell with no status reads either "Covered by" and names the row whose route it shares, or "No vector" and gives the reason the party kind has no route in that posture.

### 1.4 References

`D1`–`D21` and `OD1`–`OD18` are ADR-ETH-01's, as is `OD19`. `T`, `A1`–`A3`, `H1`–`H3`, `C1`–`C23`, `S1`, `S2`, `P1` and `P2` are ADR-ETH-02's. Section marks prefixed *kickoff* refer to `docs/KICKOFF-meta-ethics-constitution.md`.

A decision ADR-ETH-02's body replaces is cited through its replacement: D4 through C1, D7 through C15, D8 and D12 through A1 and C7, D14 through A2. The front matter of both records also lists D1 (scope of symmetry) and D9 as superseded, but ADR-ETH-02's body refines them rather than replacing them (C13; C11 and C12) and twice says D1 stands (§4 b); they are therefore cited directly. C11 and C12 refine only D9's replay of verdicts: a cell resting on replay cites "D9 as refined by C11, C12", or by the one of them it uses. D9's ineligibility of seats on matters about themselves is not refined, and a cell resting on it cites D9 alone. Of the open decisions, OD7 is closed by C15, OD16's half on the canonical metric by C10, OD19 by A2, and OD20, raised by ADR-ETH-02, by C1; OD16's half on early-warning coordinates remains open.

Attacks are numbered TM1–TM17, and a cell is cited as attack, party kind and posture, for example *TM3, orchestrator, bad will*.

## 4. Record inconsistencies observed

Mapping the cells surfaced three inconsistencies between the records, recorded here as found; the second amendment to ADR-ETH-02 changes them as noted. A fourth entry records what that amendment leaves open.

**a. Ratification state.** ADR-ETH-01's front matter and status line read *Proposed*. ADR-ETH-02's body says "ADR-ETH-01 is ratified and its body is frozen", and also refers to "D14 as ratified", "D18's trigger as ratified" and, in its provenance, to ADR-ETH-01 as ratified. ADR-ETH-02 is itself *Proposed*, so it amends a record whose ratification no front matter shows. Resolved by the second amendment: a merge places a record and ratifies nothing (C5, S1); both records stay *Proposed* and are ratified together in one act signed by both Custodians.

**b. Supersession lists against ADR-ETH-02's body.** The two front-matter lists agree with each other: D1 (scope of symmetry), D4, D7, D8, D9, D12, D14, OD7, OD16 (closed half) and OD19. The body of ADR-ETH-02 says "Replaces" only for D8 and D12 (A1), D14 and OD19 (A2), D4 (C1), and D7 and OD7 (C15). It refines D9 (C11, C12) without replacing it, and says "D1 stands" twice (A2 and C13). In the other direction, A3 replaces ADR-ETH-01's accepted cost "the formal frame is exact for finite systems", which neither list names, and C1 closes OD20, which ADR-ETH-01's ledger never held.

**c. H3 and OD6.** OD6 asks how the Custodian seat is filled at genesis. H3 fixes that one party holds every seat in the genesis regime, and ADR-ETH-01's own status line says the owner "is therefore the Custodian for this act". ADR-ETH-02's open-decisions section does not mark OD6 as touched ("the ledger of ADR-ETH-01 otherwise stands"). This matrix cites OD1–OD6 as pending for the Custodian only outside the genesis case. P1 and H3 now fill both Custodian seats at genesis and close OD1, OD2, OD3 and OD6; cells that cite those for the Custodian are re-read when the amendment is ratified, and OD4 and OD5 stay pending.

**d. Open after the second amendment.** Who answers for the non-human seat before a law that does not recognise it as a person, now ADR-ETH-02 OD23; whether an instance whose parties are all of one kind can conform (OD21), the constitution being defined for two kinds; and whether C14's undecided conflicts go to a human (OD22). Settled in the same amendment: ratification of both records in one signed act; a non-human seat's signature as both consent and attestation; D9's ineligibility read as about the party; no secrecy of the drawn judge; the seats' composition by kind declared under F6; no kind- or seat-reserved ratification of mandates (C7); and a seat's replacement beyond its own veto (P1).

## 5. What this skeleton does not yet do

It does not map attacks to constitution clauses, because no clause has been drafted; when clauses exist, each cell gains the clause that closes it, and the decision column becomes that clause's rationale. It does not yet express rows as adversarial fixtures, which the brainstorm record (§6) makes the same artefact as the fairness test specification. It covers the nine attacks the kickoff and the brainstorm name, with matrices, and eight added with the second amendment to ADR-ETH-02 (TM10–TM17), in the register only, whose matrices by party kind are not yet drawn; an attack found later is added as a new row, never folded into an existing one.
