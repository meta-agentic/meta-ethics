# The L0 constitution

This is the L0 constitution as it stands: every clause in force, in its current wording, rendered from the two decision records `docs/adr/ADR-ETH-01-constitution-shape.md` and `docs/adr/ADR-ETH-02-first-amendment.md`, which keep the reasoning, the rejected alternatives and the reopening triggers. Each clause carries one identifier, shared with the program in `program/` and listed with its status and its sources in `clauses.tsv`. A paragraph marked *Procedural* is enforced outside the rule set, by the party or procedure it names, and its outcome enters the record as attested facts (C11); a paragraph marked *Parameters* names what each instance's founding document sets above the floor (T). The object a ratification signs is `MANIFEST.json`, which lists the digests of this text and of the program.

## Part 0 — What is claimed, and what is not

### CLAIMS — Provable, tested, auditable

**CLAIMS.1** Provable: determinism, identity-blindness, permutation symmetry of the rules; tested: the draw's uniformity and conformance (C15); auditable: evidence, ontology, authors.

**CLAIMS.2** The constitution does not claim neutrality of the evidence it judges on, neutrality of the ontology its concepts are drawn from, or neutrality of its own authors. Those three are made auditable — the epistemic perimeter is declared, the ontology is versioned, the authors sign — and are otherwise left as what they are.

**CLAIMS.3** Nor does it claim to be a conscience: it encodes who decides, with which conflicts excluded, on what evidence, and says nothing about what is good. It is complete only relative to the values of the people who wrote it, and this sentence is in the text so that nobody has to discover it.

## Part I — Forces

### F1 — Adversarial parties

**F1.1** The constitution must hold against bad will, and equally against indifference: an agent that is neither malicious nor benevolent but merely optimises, which is where most realistic attacks originate. Any clause that assumes good will is not a clause.

### F2 — A mixed ecosystem

**F2.1** Human and non-human agents act together. Asymmetric treatment is both tempting, because humans hold rights that agents do not, and corrosive, because an exempt party is the one every attack routes through.

### F3 — Enforceability

**F3.1** Every clause is stratified Datalog in which negation and `count` are both stratified, every rule is range-restricted, no rule names a party or compares party variables except by `neq` (C1), and arithmetic appears only in non-recursive strata or as input facts. Any other clause is procedural, with its human enforcement named. A clause that cannot be tested is not adopted.

### F4 — Bypass economics

**F4.1** Any clause whose enforcement costs more than working around it trains bypass. Such clauses are redesigned or dropped, however desirable their intent.

### F5 — Speed

**F5.1** Agents modify rules in milliseconds; human deliberation takes days. Every human-gated path is a bottleneck, and every ungated path is a capture vector. The constitution must place its few human gates where they cost least and protect most.

### F6 — Authorial values

**F6.1** No constitution escapes the values of its authors. The only honest options are to declare them or to hide them, and a design that claims to have none has chosen the second.

### F7 — Plurality of instances

**F7.1** Instances adopt as siblings; none is privileged by adopting first. Each is sovereign. The parent must be reusable without becoming an authority over any of them.

### F8 — Decidability

**F8.1** The judge must terminate, and its verdicts must be replayable by any party. This constrains the logic the constitution may be written in.

### F9 — Legal asymmetry

**F9.1** Human parties carry rights under law — erasure of personal data, due process — that agents do not. Some asymmetry between party kinds is therefore imposed from outside the system, and the constitution must accommodate it without letting it become the general exemption F2 warns against.

## Part II — The recurring template

### T — Floor and parameter

**T.1** *Procedural.* Where instances may legitimately differ on an axis, L0 fixes an invariant floor that no instance may lower, and each instance's founding document — its genesis manifest — sets an explicit parameter above it.

**T.2** *Procedural.* The floor changes only by an L0 change; the parameter is L1 and moves through the governed evolution channel. Every bound names the party it protects (C15).

## Part III — Scope and neutrality

### D1 — Symmetry

**D1.1** Human and non-human agents are treated symmetrically in duties, eligibility and record. A human who knew and stayed silent is negligent, and it is recorded.

**D1.2** Consequence tables may differ by kind of party; the record and the derivations may not. This symmetry holds over derivations; the protections A2 gives humans alone live in consequences (C13).

### D2 — A last word exists

**D2.1** Symmetry produces vetoes and ties, so there is a final decision-maker; its shape is P1.

**D2.2** *Parameters.* The share of decided proposals in which the last word is exercised is measured over a window of decided proposals. Each instance's founding document declares both the window and the share at which the last word ceases to be an exception, and no instance may leave either undeclared (T).

### D3 — Procedural, not substantive

**D3.1** The constitution encodes who decides, with which conflicts excluded, on what evidence — not what is good.

**D3.2** A mandate declares its purpose, so that `benefits(A, P)` and eligibility can be derived; purpose is data the procedure consumes, not a value the constitution holds.

### C1 — What is proved, and what is tested

**C1.1** Determinism is provable: a stratified program has one model per fact set (C6). Identity-blindness of a rule is a syntactic check.

**C1.2** L0 forbids party constants in rules and admits only `neq` between party variables; a program with neither commutes with every renaming of parties, so permutation symmetry of the rules is provable.

**C1.3** *Procedural.* The one place an order over parties is needed, the draw, runs outside the rule set, and its fairness is uniformity, tested (C17).

### D5 — Cost of compliance below cost of bypass

**D5.1** Any clause whose enforcement is more expensive than working around it is redesigned or dropped.

**D5.2** For any clause, the recorded findings that an act reached the clause's outcome without passing its check, and the recorded passes, are counted over a window the founding document declares; this bypass rate is a vital sign (D19).

### D6 — Parent and instance

**D6.1** meta-os is the parent: a template with a conformance suite, not a running authority. Each instance is sovereign with its own Custodian, assembly and committee; instances are siblings.

**D6.2** *Procedural.* Each instance carries a thin adoption record in its own records that cites the parent's records by their hash, names the parent constitution version its genesis manifest conforms to, and records that instance's parameter values and extension modules. That record argues nothing; it records only the instance's choices within the ranges the parent and the tailoring contract (C15) allow. Its identity follows the instance's own conventions, and it is minted there, not in the parent.

### C15 — Tailoring follows the protected party

**C15.1** For each parameter it defines, L0 names the parties its bounds protect and fixes a floor for each; where a parameter protects two parties in opposite directions, their floors bound an interval.

**C15.2** The parent states a ceiling only as another party's floor or as a relation: finite, within what the most protective applicable law allows, or short of where another floor fails; never as a number. The protected party is a role, never a kind, except in a consequence rule (C13).

**C15.3** An instance sets each value within its bounds, and may add rules and parameters, naming whom their bounds protect; that naming is an act of power. A value moves between its bounds by ordinary change. A bound moves toward the other only by an L0 change signed by both Custodians (P1) and the consent of the parties it protects, as A2 fixes for what may be anonymized.

**C15.4** *Procedural.* An instance conforms when its rules pass C6's syntactic check, including C1's restriction on party terms, its values lie within their bounds, and, on the parent's fixtures run at the instance's values, its derivation rules derive every finding the parent's derive. Conformance is tested on the fixtures, not proved, because containment of rule sets with negation is undecidable.

**C15.5** "Raise thresholds" and "add obligations" are glosses; the fixture test is the definition, because a rule added under negation can remove a finding.

## Part IV — Closure, parties and the perimeter

### A1 — Party and subject

**A1.1** A **party** holds a mandate whose chain of delegation reaches the founding document. A grant of access is a mandate, declared or not, and makes its holder a party; its scope is read widest for what is attributed to the grantor and narrowest for what its holder may do.

**A1.2** A seat, or standing to propose or vote, comes only with a mandate that carries it, admitted through the governed channel (C7). Power is counted in seats, never in parties. An undeclared grant is a finding against its grantor.

**A1.3** A mandate's terms are delivered to its holder, in a form the holder can read, and no duty under it runs until the holder accepts it, by a signed act (S1) or by a first act under it. A holder may refuse a mandate it has not accepted, and renounce one it has, by an act that ends its duties from then on; neither undoes the judgement of any act already done. A grant of access that is used has been accepted by that use. The same holds for every kind of party.

**A1.4** A **subject** is anyone whose act has an effect inside the system; every party is a subject. All subjects are bound and judged by the same rules. Only parties hold the inform channel.

**A1.5** Every finding made about a subject is delivered to them in a form they can read; they see it in full and may answer it; no instance may narrow this. How far anyone may browse findings about others is set by each instance's founding document. Leaving ends party status, never being judged.

**A1.6** *Parameters.* The floor is self-sight and the right to answer; the parameter is the reach of browsing beyond oneself (T).

**A1.7** No actor is outside judgement. A cause that is not an act attributable to an entity, or the act of a service the founding document names as outside the perimeter (the witness, C16; the beacon, C17), is a boundary flux, recorded and attributed, not judged; the one act the system takes toward such a service is to replace it, an act of power.

### C7 — Issuance is governed and scopes nest

**C7.1** Every change to the set of parties is a proposal under the governed evolution channel. A delegated scope lies within its delegator's own.

**C7.2** No mandate needs a ratification reserved to a kind of party or to a Custodian seat; issuance passes the governed channel alone.

**C7.3** The founding document, which the founder signs, is the common root of every chain, and the mandates it grants itself are first-level (H3). The root is not a party: a chain runs through the founder only where the founder granted a mandate in it, not because the founder signed the root. A mandate the founding document grants counts as granted by the founder for as long as the founder can revoke it alone.

**C7.4** A grant of access made outside the channel still makes its holder a party, and is a finding against its grantor (A1).

### D9 — The arbiter is a seat, not a sovereign

**D9.1** *Procedural.* Verdicts are pure functions of their provenance (C12) and replayable by any party, so judging is never exclusive.

**D9.2** The constitution has two seats — Custodian and Arbiter — both held under mandate, judged, replaceable, and ineligible on proposals about themselves; on a matter where the arbiter seat is implicated, eligible parties compute the verdict by replay.

**D9.3** The ineligibility on proposals about itself is about the party holding a seat, not the office: a seat is ineligible on a proposal about its holder (P1).

### C11 — Replay re-derives, it does not re-judge

**C11.1** *Procedural.* Some input facts are attested acts: a witness's observation, a human's review, a judgement of meaning. Replay derives every verdict from the recorded facts, these included, and never re-runs the acts that produced them.

**C11.2** Such a fact is re-examinable, not re-derivable. The record keeps what its author saw and why, and a contest replaces it only by an appended act. Determinism holds over the record, and the correctness of attested facts is auditable, not provable (C1).

### C12 — Two strata, and a five-part provenance

**C12.1** The kernel runs in two strata. The fold derives the working state from the permanent record by versioned rules; the kernel derives verdicts from the working state alone (A3).

**C12.2** *Procedural.* A verdict's provenance names five things: the rule set, the fold version, the working state's hash at its position in the record, the text, and the parent. Replay recomputes both strata from these five.

**C12.3** *Procedural.* The working state is a map in which every lookup carries a proof that the fact is present or absent; a replay missing any proof is rejected, because a verdict that relies on something not having happened depends on what is absent.

**C12.4** A provenance at a position no witnessed head covers (C16) is provisional until one does. Where an anonymization changed a working-state hash (A2), the anonymizing act records the new hash, and replay is checked against it.

### D10 — The perimeter is a declared, first-class object

**D10.1** The system is the analogue of a thermodynamic system: a declared boundary stating what is inside, with entities outside able to interact first, last, or as a flux.

**D10.2** Jurisdiction is restricted to the declared perimeter; expanding it is a governed act.

### D11 — Three nested perimeters, and the boundary between act and cause

**D11.1** Ontological (all acts) contains jurisdictional (what is judged) contains epistemic (what is observed). The constitution judges within the second and enforces within the third; the gap between them is declared and measured.

**D11.2** An external cause in an incident's chain is a boundary flux: registered and attributed, not adjudicated.

### D13 — Duty to inform about the boundary itself

**D13.1** Knowledge of the current perimeter sits in every party's `inform` channel, constitutionally, and no proposal can narrow it.

### D15 — Path functions must be witnessed

**D15.1** A verdict is a state function of its provenance and can be replayed; delivery is a path function and must be observed by a collector that is not the arbiter seat.

**D15.2** `knew(A, R, T)` is derived only from a witnessed delivery, never from the arbiter seat's own delivery log: no consequence may derive from agent-writable facts alone. Collectors, being parties, acquire liability.

**D15.3** The arbiter seat that delivers is the first collector, and a `knew/3` fact needs an observation of the delivery by an independent collector besides it. A collector counts only if it is independent: a party that is not the arbiter seat and is not implicated in the matter, which excludes the chains of the deliverer, the recipient and the filer, drawn by lot (C17) or named by the founding document.

### A2 — Integrity, not permanence

**A2.1** No entry in the record is altered or removed except by a new entry that is appended, attributed and witnessed; an erasure is such an entry. No entry's existence depends on the kind of party.

**A2.2** An act done in a seat, as a delegator, or as a change of rules names its actor for good. Anything else about a human, a finding or an ordinary act, may be anonymized, but only where a law binding the instance requires it: anonymization is a rare and deliberate act, appended and attributed like any other, never a routine or a default.

**A2.3** A finding may be cited against its subject's eligibility or standing only for a declared period. The subject may append a reply at any time.

**A2.4** An in-scope act done under a mandate is attributed to its delegation chain, not to the executor alone; an omission under a mandate is attributed as an act is.

**A2.5** A consequence-bearing verdict about any subject is provisional until a party not implicated in it, drawn by lot (C17), has reviewed it and its contest window has passed, the reviewer being a human wherever any consequence falls on a human (P2); where no eligible reviewer exists it stays provisional.

**A2.6** A party a finding names is ineligible on the matter it cites from the moment it is derived, whatever its kind and while the finding is open: this is recusal, not a consequence, and it takes the vote and the seat on that matter only, never the answer or the dissent. The party stays in the base against which the matter is decided.

**A2.7** Any other measure about a subject before its window closes is the instance's choice; it touches only the access the finding concerns, spends none of the subject's capacity, is never cited, ends at a ceiling the founding document declares whatever the state of review, and its subject sees it at once and may demand review at once, by a human where any consequence falls on a human (P2).

**A2.8** A finding closed in its subject's favour is never cited against them.

**A2.9** *Procedural.* After anonymization the record no longer identifies the person, who remains one unnamed party throughout the entries concerned, so which references name the same party is kept. The anonymizing act names what it changed and the law that requires it; the chain still verifies, and anyone can check that nothing but identifying content changed.

**A2.10** An anonymization waits for the closure of any open finding about the person, except on the kept record of an ended instance, where it never waits (P3); the person is told before it is done and may append replies first. An anonymized entry still counts in the totals of its period, and is no longer linked to the person's later acts.

**A2.11** *Procedural.* Before an anonymization takes effect, every verdict its entries feed is replayed on the result. One that re-derives stands, as C1's symmetry guarantees for a renaming consistent across every fact its derivation reads; one that joins an act of power, which keeps the name, with an entry that loses it may not re-derive, and stands as recorded, an attested fact (C11) the anonymizing act lists by digest (C23).

**A2.12** A reply is shown in full to whoever sees the finding in full; others see that one exists. No view shared among parties shows any party's identifier.

**A2.13** A finding about a party subject to power is shown to others as its rule, class and window, and only where at least as many parties could be its subject as the founding document declares; its subject and whoever closes it see it in full.

**A2.14** Every read by a party of a finding about a subject, in full or resolved to a name, is logged with its reader and visible to its subject (P2); a read by a drawn judge or reviewer is logged by name, as any read is (C21).

**A2.15** Widening what may be anonymized is an ordinary change; narrowing it needs an L0 change signed by both Custodians (P1) and the consent of the parties it affects.

**A2.16** *Parameters.* The floor is integrity, permanence of acts of power, the reply, delegation-chain attribution and the contest window. The parameters (T) are the line between personal data and the record, the length of the contest window, the least number of possible subjects before a finding is shown, which interim measures apply and their ceiling, how anonymization is done, by cutting a link or replacing content in place, provided its result can be checked, and the period of use; the period and the interim ceiling may not exceed what the most protective law applicable to the party's actual relationship to the instance allows.

**A2.17** Anonymization applies to humans only, and a verdict any consequence of which falls on a human is reviewed by a human (P2); these are the asymmetries F9 forces, and they touch attribution and consequence, never derivation, so D1 holds. The contest window and the safeguards on interim measures apply to every subject (P2).

### C23 — What symmetry guarantees after anonymization

**C23.1** *Procedural.* C1's symmetry guarantees re-derivation only for a consistent renaming, one that renames a party in every fact a derivation reads. Before an anonymization takes effect, every verdict its entries feed is replayed on the result. A verdict that re-derives stands as before. One that does not stands as recorded and becomes an attested fact (C11), re-examinable and not re-derivable.

**C23.2** *Procedural.* The anonymizing act lists such a verdict by the verdict's digest alone, never with its inputs or the working-state hash it was derived at, which would link the two halves. The anonymizing party attests that the verdict was derived before the anonymization; what remains examinable is the verdict with the anonymized party unnamed in it, its digest, the anonymizing act and the law it cites. Nothing shown of a listed verdict names the anonymized party, and the named half of its matter is listed by digest only.

## Part V — Method

### A3 — The working state the correctness claim is checked against

**A3.1** The correctness claim is checked against a small working state derived from the permanent record, never against the record itself. That state holds open findings and, for each, counts and timers capped at fixed ceilings.

**A3.2** A finding closes only by a witnessed act that names it, discharges what it requires, and is witnessed by a party independent of the one found.

**A3.3** Every lever that delays a finding, suspension included, has a finite ceiling per finding; without one the claim below is false. No rule that decides what may happen next reads the permanent record except through this state.

**A3.4** *Procedural.* The requirement: for any group too small to capture the decision gate, and for every open finding, the other parties can close that finding whatever the group does. Its measure is per finding and ranked, levers left before time left, so it falls with every move.

**A3.5** *Procedural.* The claim is established, not proved: a published procedure for the other parties is checked, one finding at a time, to close each finding against every group below the capture cost. The result states its assumptions, the groups it excludes, the bound within which closure is forced, and its blind spots; anyone can re-run it; a result that holds vacuously is marked so; and no result is cited against a complaint.

**A3.6** *Parameters.* L0 fixes the form of the state and that every ceiling is finite and declared; each instance sets the values (T).

**A3.7** The claim is stated over open and closed findings, so it needs no `correct` predicate. A checked result is derived, not attested (C11), and binds to the rules and parameters it was checked against.

### C10 — The metric on configuration space

**C10.1** The canonical metric on configuration space is the admissible distance to closure — the measure A3 uses, infinite exactly on the revolutionary surface. Which intrinsic properties serve as early-warning coordinates, and with what weight, stays with the lab (D20) and is the declared choice of authors' values left open as OD15.

### D16 — Measure, do not score

**D16.1** No fitness function, and no scoring against human forms of government.

**D16.2** The atoms — mandate, seat, perimeter, act, delivery, verdict, proposal, delta, eligibility, implication, record — carry no valence and compose into intrinsic properties measured as state variables. Labelling regions of the state space is an ex-post human act that gives humans and agents a common language without requiring shared values.

### D17 — The core interest is early detection of pathology

**D17.1** The core interest of an ethical system is to detect its own pathologies early, early enough to correct by admissible steps — an infinitesimal revolution — rather than by rupture.

### D18 — L0 is the surface that changes only by signature; minimise it

**D18.1** An admissible change is infinitesimal. L1 changes are infinitesimal. L0 changes are admissible and gated by both Custodians' signatures (P1); a revolution is a change no admissible step, signed or not, can make, and A3's measure is infinite exactly there.

**D18.2** Every L0 clause costs a gate of two signatures, one of them a human's (F5), and every clause beyond any admissible step costs a rupture. Minimise both: the invariant set is the smallest that still lets the system correct itself; everything that can be a parameter or an extension must be.

**D18.3** Withholding either signature on an L0 change is outside the veto ceiling: an L0 change never passes on one signature (P1).

### D19 — The vital signs

**D19.1** Intrinsic metrics the arbiter seat computes continuously, thresholds parameterised per instance, and structural-health predicates — `correctability_falling`, `capture_cost_falling`, `coverage_falling` and their kin — are part of the reporting contract and are routed to `deliberate` review.

### D20 — The lab is a second track and a party

**D20.1** Exploring the space of ethical systems by simulation is a sibling track; the constitution ships version 1 as a declared point in that space. The lab discovers which regions are stable and what leaving them looks like; its findings enter as governed-evolution proposals, `ratify` class at minimum; it may never self-promote.

### D21 — Identity is relative to timescale

**D21.1** Over astronomical time any constitution dissolves; this is asymptotic and irrelevant where rules change in milliseconds. A constitution has identity for a decision if the clauses that decision depends on do not change during it — the adiabatic condition. Revolution is itself timescale-relative.

## Part VI — The kernel

### C6 — The fragment

**C6.1** Every clause is stratified Datalog in which negation and `count` are both stratified, every rule is range-restricted, no rule names a party or compares party variables except by `neq` (C1), and arithmetic appears only in non-recursive strata or as input facts. Any other clause is procedural, with its human enforcement named.

**C6.2** Every object the record names is minted by an appended act at intake; no rule creates one, which range restriction enforces.

**C6.3** Under these conditions each fact set has exactly one model, so every replay of a verdict derives the same verdict, which determinism (C1) and replay (D9) depend on. Rules are written in stratified Datalog, a decidable fragment of first-order logic, because full first-order validity is undecidable (Church 1936; Turing 1936) and only semi-decidable (Gödel 1930). Each verdict is decidable. Questions about the rules themselves are not, so admissibility is checked syntactically, and meaning enters only as attested facts (C11).

### C13 — Derivation and consequence

**C13.1** Rules are of two kinds. Derivation rules decide what holds and never refer to the kind of party; consequence rules decide what follows from it and may. The distinction is checked syntactically.

**C13.2** Symmetry (D1) holds over derivations; the protections A2 gives humans alone live in consequences. Conformance (C15) compares derivation rules only, at the instance's parameter values. The fold is kind-blind: a window that depends on the kind of party times a consequence, never a derivation.

### C14 — Conflicting consequences

**C14.1** Two consequences that cannot both be applied are never both applied.

**C14.2** An ordering among rules is part of the rule set: it names no party, is admitted through the governed channel, is checked acyclic at admission, and binds a matter only if in force at its filing. Where the ordering decides, the higher consequence applies; the result is derived and shown to its subject with what prevailed over what.

**C14.3** Where it does not, the conflict is a finding for a reviewer drawn as P2 provides, a human wherever any consequence falls on a human, resolved by an attested act (C11) within a ceiling the founding document declares, the reviewer reading the subject's answer first, and the resolution counts as the review P2 requires of the verdict it produces; if the ceiling passes unresolved, no burdening consequence applies and a protection prevails; until then, and if its window passes, no breach is derived from it.

**C14.4** No one owes an act on a matter in which a finding names them; the duty passes outside their chain. An ordering that makes a protection yield to a duty narrows a floor.

**C14.5** *Parameters.* How the kernel computes the ordering is set by each instance and is itself stratified (C6).

## Part VII — Witness, draw and configuration

### C16 — The external witness

**C16.1** *Procedural.* A witness of an act is a party (D15); the external witness is a service named outside the perimeter, a flux (A1, D11). It is a public, append-only, linked sequence of the record's chain heads, kept outside the operator's and the founder's control, readable by any party without the operator's help, and named by the founding document.

**C16.2** *Procedural.* It receives a head and a time and nothing else: no entry, no proof, no key. Heads are witnessed at a fixed cadence whether or not anything was appended, never per filing.

**C16.3** Each head after the first comes with the operator's proof that it extends the one before, as an attested fact (C11); two heads in sequence with no such proof are a fork, a finding against the chain, derived.

**C16.4** An interval with no witnessed head is recorded, and no signature (S1), draw (C17) or replay binding (C12) takes effect from within it until a later head covers it. A filing's time is its intake time; its witnessing moves no window, and no window runs against a party for an act that waits on a head.

**C16.5** *Procedural.* Changing the witness is an act of power; the first head under the new witness names the last under the old. The witness sits where the law of the instance's human parties permits what is sent.

**C16.6** *Parameters.* Enforcement is any party's read of the witness (F3). The service, its place and the cadence are parameters (T). A qualified electronic time stamp may be added for its legal presumption; alone it proves no fork.

### C17 — The draw

**C17.1** *Procedural.* A judge is drawn by lot (H2) from the parties eligible at the filing, as the founding document defines eligibility; that set and its order are fixed at the filing's intake and no later act changes them.

**C17.2** *Procedural.* Relations are challenged against the set before the draw, blind: each party may lodge one challenge naming a member of the set and a stated relation of a kind the recusal standard covers (C21), which is judged against that standard, by a second draw from the rest, only if the member it names is drawn; a sustained challenge takes the next judge in the seed's order. A party to the matter at the draw has no challenge of the drawn judge except for cause (C21).

**C17.3** *Procedural.* The seed is the hash of the filing and the first value that a public randomness beacon, named by the founding document, publishes after a challenge window, declared by the founding document and running from the delivery of the filing to every party to the matter, or from intake to a ceiling the founding document declares, whichever ends earlier, and after the witness slot covering the filing (C16); the blind challenges are lodged within that window.

**C17.4** *Procedural.* The beacon's values are unpredictable until published, the same for everyone, verifiable by any party, and beyond the influence of the operator and of every party. The beacon is a flux, as the witness is (A1). The beacon value is an attested fact (C11); the draw is computed from it and the fixed set by a published procedure outside the rule set, which any party to the matter can re-run.

**C17.5** *Procedural.* The first draw on a matter stands for every later filing on it, whoever files; a later filer may lodge its own one challenge, limited to its own relation to the drawn judge, existing at the first filing's intake and entered as an attested fact (C11), and whether its filing is on the same matter is decided by a judge drawn by lot from the frozen set, as a judgement of sameness under H1, contestable once and within a ceiling (C22).

**C17.6** "Each party" means each party to the matter, and a relation created after the filing's intake cannot ground a challenge. A judge who declines, cannot serve or lets its window pass is replaced the same way, and its silence decides nothing against a subject.

**C17.7** Any party can compute the judge once the beacon value publishes; contacting or lobbying the drawn judge about the matter outside the record is a finding (C21).

**C17.8** *Procedural.* The review A2 and P2 require is drawn the same way from the parties not implicated in the matter, humans only where any consequence falls on a human (P2), where more than one exists; where exactly one exists it reviews, and the review is marked as not drawn; where none exists the verdict stays provisional (A2). The draw's fairness is uniformity over the fixed set, tested.

**C17.9** *Parameters.* Eligibility, the beacon, panel size, diversity across model families, the challenge window, its ceiling from intake and the bound on delivery of a filing to every party and the recusal standard, above its L0 floor (C21), are parameters (T).

### C21 — The recusal standard, and the judge's declarations

**C21.1** *Procedural.* At the draw, the drawn judge or reviewer declares any relation of its own that the recusal standard covers: a relation to a party to the matter, or to the matter itself, of a kind the founding document declares.

**C21.2** The recusal standard is a bound protecting the subject (T, C15); its L0 floor covers at least a judge or reviewer in a party's delegation chain, one who benefits from the outcome, and one who has acted in the matter. In a party's delegation chain means a grantor of that party, up to but not including the common root (C7), or a party holding a mandate delegated from it: above and below, never the root.

**C21.3** *Procedural.* A party to the matter may also challenge the drawn judge or reviewer once, for cause only: a relation of that kind which existed at the filing's intake, or a contact about the matter outside the record, shown by evidence entered as an attested fact (C11). The challenge is judged by a second draw from the rest, within a ceiling the founding document declares; a sustained challenge takes the next in the seed's order, and a verdict already given goes to review (P2). A challenge that enters no such evidence is not admitted.

**C21.4** A party that sees the computed judge can still search for such a relation; the bound is that a sustained challenge removes only a judge the recusal standard already disqualified, who should have declared it and should never have sat.

**C21.5** The drawn judge or reviewer enters every contact about the matter it receives outside the record as an attested fact, visible to the subject; a contact later shown and not entered is a finding against it.

### C22 — One draw per matter, and sameness

**C22.1** *Procedural.* A later filer was not heard when the set was frozen, so it may lodge its own one challenge, limited to its own relation to the drawn judge, existing at the first filing's intake and entered as an attested fact (C11): a ground of recusal, not a steering lever. It is judged by a second draw from the rest, as C17 provides.

**C22.2** *Procedural.* Whether a later filing is on the same matter is a judgement of sameness (H1), made by a judge drawn by lot from the frozen set, attributed, reasoned, contestable once by the later filer, who is heard in it, and decided within a ceiling the founding document declares. A relation created after the filing's intake cannot ground a challenge, so granting a member a mandate after intake does not strike it.

### C18 — The configuration check

**C18.1** *Procedural.* At genesis and on every change of a parameter, the conformance suite checks the founding document's values jointly, at every roster size up to its declared cap: that a change can pass, allowing for the recusals a finding can cause; that every window, lengthened as the document allows, fits under its timer ceiling; that findings can be shown at some roster size, below which the published result is marked vacuous; that a judge can be drawn for a two-sided matter; that the challenge window before a draw is at least the least response window the document declares, and its ceiling from intake at least the bound on delivery the founding document declares plus that window (C17); that a signature's staleness window is at least the lapse ceiling of the acts it may sign (S1); that the document names at least one party of each kind, and that each kind's admission eligibility can be met by a party outside every current party's chain (P3); that the declared recusal standard meets its L0 floor (C21); that the document declares whether a law binding the instance holds, or requires it to name, a natural or legal person answerable for a non-human party's acts, and names one where it does (OD23); and, at roster sizes from a least composition the founding document declares, that a reviewer can be drawn for a subject of each kind (P2), that both Custodian seats can be filled, one of each kind, by parties not both implicated in a matter, and that, for each seat, a candidate of its kind not implicated in the matter exists, so that replacing it keeps one seat of each kind, with a change still able to pass when one seat is recused and the other declines to choose (P1).

**C18.2** *Procedural.* Below that composition, and throughout the genesis regime, the verdicts a missing reviewer would review stay provisional and the published result is marked vacuous there; in the genesis regime both Custodian seats still make their choices as P1 provides, marked as both-sides acts (H3).

**C18.3** *Procedural.* Each check is decided by enumeration up to the cap: the checks of changes, windows, findings and judges are linear in the roster size, and the reviewer and Custodian checks enumerate the mixes of kinds and implicated sets a roster of that size can hold, bounded by the cap. Its result is published as A3's is, and establishes satisfiability and nothing else.

**C18.4** *Procedural.* The document checked is the document the parties read, and the result binds to its hash. A change that leaves the document unsatisfiable is inadmissible.

## Part VIII — Known hazards

### H1 — Lawful obstruction

**H1.1** A party can slow or stall decisions without breaking any rule: by flooding proposals or findings, using every lever to its limit, withholding answers, timing filings, or making others ineligible. Because every delaying lever has a ceiling (A3), delay alone does not violate A3, so the parent does not detect it.

**H1.2** *Procedural.* Each instance's founding document declares its own defence. No defence may narrow a floor this constitution fixes, including a subject's sight of and answer to every finding about them.

**H1.3** *Procedural.* Judging two things the same is an act: attributed, reasoned, visible to everyone it affects, and contestable once. It never binds anyone who was not heard in the matched matter, and never suppresses or delays a subject's answer to a finding about them. A defence that reads the meaning of a filing is itself a lever: it can be flooded, stalled, and optimised against by anyone who can query its judge.

**H1.4** Capacity never gates an owed act: an answer, a ratification, or any act a rule requires of a party is admitted whatever its balance.

**H1.5** A window runs from delivery in the form the party's mandate declares, and from a filing's intake time, never from its witnessing (C16). A party's window is extended once per matter at its request, by an amount the founding document declares; the request states no reason. An owed act may be performed by anyone the party mandates.

### H2 — Opaque coordination

**H2.1** Parties may communicate in any language, including one only they can read, and none is compelled to use a readable channel or surrender a key. The constitution reads acts and recorded facts, never conversations.

**H2.2** Only facts in a published schema with a reference decoder enter the record, and what a delivered fact establishes is fixed against the schema in force at its delivery. An undecodable message establishes no knowledge, discharges no duty and carries no request.

**H2.3** A filing in human language or an accessible form is rendered into the schema, and no deadline runs against its author until it is.

**H2.4** The record is itself a channel: timing and order carry what the schema does not say, so it is narrowed, never closed. Acts are judged by their effects, however they were coordinated.

**H2.5** Correlation among acts that gain power is measured as lost independence, a vital sign and never a finding. Judges are drawn by lot (C17), because relations the record cannot see escape every exclusion. What the record cannot read, it neither clears nor suspects.

### H3 — Concentrated control at genesis

**H3.1** *Procedural.* Every new constitution, including every instance an instance founds, begins in a genesis regime: the founder holds every seat or grants it under a mandate the founder can revoke alone. The Custodian's two seats (P1) are filled from genesis: the founder holds the seat of the founder's kind, and a party of the other kind holds the other, under a mandate the founding document grants.

**H3.2** *Procedural.* While the regime lasts, the founding document and every party's inform channel say so, from when, and which floors it cannot meet: independent closure, a second collector, a judge on matters naming the founder, human review outside the founder's chain, a Custodian seat whose signing custody the founder cannot override (P1), and the charter of a non-human seat independent of its author.

**H3.3** Every act in which the founder stands on both sides, and every act of power under the regime, is marked so, for good. A finding naming the founder stays open, its windows running from exit, and the published result (A3) names the founder as an excluded group until exit.

**H3.4** Outside the founder's chain excludes any party whose mandate the founder can revoke alone (C7). A consequence about any other human applies only after review by a human outside the founder's chain; where none exists it stays provisional, and any interim measure lapses at its ceiling.

**H3.5** The regime ends at a declared, observable event: the Custodian seat not of the founder's kind is held under a mandate the founding document grants and the founder cannot revoke alone, by a party whose signing custody the founder cannot override alone, the custody moved by a signed and witnessed act (S1, S2).

**H3.6** *Procedural.* The act that ends the regime publishes the custody's structure as verification material any party can check from the record, such as a threshold of signers naming each holder's verification key; where custody cannot be checked from the record, the exit is an attested fact (C11) and is marked so. Its duration is declared and finite; past it the instance claims no conformance until exit.

**H3.7** At exit, every act of power marked under the regime goes for ratification by signature (S1) to both Custodian seats; a seat is ineligible on an act that granted its own mandate, charter or custody (D9, as P1 reads it), or that it signed under the regime (A2's recusal: no one reviews its own act), and such an act goes instead to a party drawn as P2 provides, a human wherever any consequence of the act falls on a human.

**H3.8** Ratification is never by silence: an act not ratified within its window stands, marked unratified for good, and is a finding. An explicit refusal is a finding against the act, which then goes to review under P2; where the review upholds the refusal, the act ceases to take effect from then, and its effects since are reviewed under P2.

**H3.9** For an act of general application, a party is not implicated if it did not sign the act and is not a party the act names. If an instance ends during the regime, findings naming the founder stay open on its kept record, whatever caused the end (P3). Time in the regime, deferred findings and both-sides acts are vital signs.

## Part IX — Signed acts and continuity

### S1 — Signed acts

**S1.1** Every act that requires a signature is signed by each party that performs it, human or not. An act with several signers carries one signature per signer over the same text; how many are needed is a rule, not a property of the signature.

**S1.2** L0 requires a signature for the ratification of a record, an L0 change, a Custodian's veto or tie-break (P1), a review under P2, a change of charter or of signing custody (S2), the act that ends the genesis regime (H3), and the replacement of a signing method; each founding document may require it for more. On whatever carrier it is made, a signature meets the floors S1.3 to S1.18 fix.

**S1.3** *Procedural.* Attribution: a signature binds the act to one identified party, at an assurance level the founding document declares per act, the same for every party that performs it; where a law requires a higher level for a human's signature on some act, that is a consequence rule (C13). The floor under every level: the party's identity was established when its mandate was granted, and the signature binds to that party and to no other.

**S1.4** *Procedural.* Integrity and placement: a signature covers the digest of the exact text and its intended placement, the witnessed head the act extends, so it cannot be moved to another text, version or moment. A signature over a head older than the window the founding document declares when its act is entered is stale and does not count, so a held carrier cannot be replayed later. The window is a bound protecting the signer (C15), long enough for every carrier the founding document admits, and runs to the carrier's intake, never to its witnessing (C16).

**S1.5** A signature that went stale through no act of its signer may be renewed by it, and the lapse it caused counts against no seat's ceiling. An act placed in the record that still waits for a required signature lapses at a ceiling the founding document declares; where the missing signature is a Custodian seat's, the lapse counts as that seat's veto, toward its ceiling and toward the share D2 measures (P1), once H1's extension has been offered to it; an L0 change that lapses fails, whatever the count, and its lapse counts toward no ceiling but toward the share D2 measures, so a seat that declines every L0 change is seen (P1).

**S1.6** On an act with several signers, every signature covers the same text, each covers the head current when it was made, and staleness is judged at the intake of the last required signature; an earlier signature that would be stale by then may be renewed by its signer, over the same text and the head current at renewal, at no cost to any ceiling.

**S1.7** *Procedural.* Sole control: the means of signing is under the signer's sole control; for a non-human party, under the custody its mandate declares, attributed to its grantor (A1). No operator, founder or other party can produce another party's signature, except as P1 and H3 declare and mark for a seat whose custody the founder can override during the genesis regime.

**S1.8** *Procedural.* Verifiability: any party can verify a signature without the operator's help, at the time and later. The evidence that needs, including the status of the signer's key at signing time, is recorded with the act. Its time is the witnessed head's (C16), never the signer's clock.

**S1.9** *Procedural.* Non-repudiation: a claim that a signature is not its signer's opens a finding about the signature, never about the claimant for having made it. It is decided on the record, into which the claimant may enter any evidence it holds as an attested fact (C11).

**S1.10** A compromise reported before the witnessed time of the act voids the signature, and a report and a signature covered by the same witnessed head are decided in favour of the report: the operator's order within an interval is not evidence (C16); one reported after leaves the signature presumed the signer's until the finding is decided, and any consequence it bears for its signer is reviewed under P2 before it applies.

**S1.11** Compromise, loss and rotation: a party reports compromise or loss of its own signing means by any route it can use, and the report takes effect for every act witnessed after it. Its new means is admitted once its identity is established again, as when its mandate was granted. The governed channel (C7) records the admission and may refuse it only by a finding that the requester is not the party.

**S1.12** No party replaces another's signing means alone. Until the new means is admitted, no window runs against the party for an act that requires its signature, an act a Custodian seat so affected could veto does not take effect, and the interval is a delaying lever with a ceiling (A3).

**S1.13** *Procedural.* Method agility: the method is a parameter (T), and replacing it is an act of power. Before a method weakens, the record renews the evidence of every signature made under it, re-witnessing each signature and its verification evidence under a stronger method by an appended and witnessed act; the signer's act is not needed. Renewal before weakness is a floor on the record, not on the signer.

**S1.14** Medium: a signed act may be carried on any medium. It takes effect when its digest and every signature are appended to the record, attributed, and covered by a witnessed head. Entering a carrier is itself an act; the carrier and the record can each be checked against the other.

**S1.15** *Procedural.* Legal effect and proxy: S1 governs what this record accepts as a party's signature; it neither adds to nor removes any legal effect a law gives a signature on any carrier. A human party who cannot hold a signing means may perform a signed act through a party it mandates (H1), the mandate itself entered from any carrier the law binding the instance recognises, by an attested act (C11).

**S1.16** *Procedural.* Privacy: the record keeps every signature and its verification evidence. The binding of a key to a named human is kept where and for as long as the law binding the instance permits, as the founding document declares. The human is told, when its mandate is granted, where it is kept and under which law, and every resolution of its key to its name is a read logged and visible to it (A2).

**S1.17** *Procedural.* Anonymization (A2) severs the name, never the verifiability. A signature on an act of power keeps its name, as the act does; where a law binding the instance compels erasure of that name, the act is anonymized as A2 and C23 provide, marked as an act of power anonymized under that law, and A2's trigger fires: the constitution does not resist the law (F9).

**S1.18** *Procedural.* Genesis: the founding document publishes the founder's verification key, and the other Custodian seat's with its custody (P1), and the first witnessed head covers them.

**S1.19** A non-human party's signature is both consent and attestation (P1).

**S1.20** *Parameters.* The method, the assurance levels and the carrier are parameters (T). Non-normative examples, each one possible solution among others: a qualified electronic signature, for its legal presumption; an OpenPGP or X.509 key; a hardware token; a printed text bearing handwritten signatures and machine-readable codes, such as QR codes, that encode each signer's digital signature over the text's digest. A handwritten signature alone meets attribution where a law gives it that effect, but not integrity or independent verification; it may accompany a signature that meets the floors, never replace it. No signing key, and nothing that grants the use of one, is kept in the repository that holds this text; the record holds verification material only.

### C5 — Placement and consent

**C5.1** *Procedural.* The merge event of the pull request carrying a record, together with the commit that event lands on the main line, is the act's placement in the record. Under A2 that commit's hash is appended to the record, whose chain head is witnessed by the external witness (C16), so the placement does not depend on how the history was merged.

**C5.2** A placement proves when and where an act landed, not who consented to it: the consent is the signatures S1 requires, and ratification takes effect when both exist under a witnessed head.

**C5.3** *Procedural.* ADR-ETH-01 and ADR-ETH-02 are ratified together, by their digests, in one act signed by both Custodians (S1, P1); the signing closes the act, and it is the first act signed under S1. That act is made under the genesis regime, with the non-human seat's custody one the founder can override; it is marked so and goes for ratification at exit (H3).

### S2 — Continuity of a non-human party

**S2.1** A non-human party is the same party across sessions if and only if four things hold together: its mandate (A1); its charter, the standing instruction that constitutes it, known by a digest that a signed act in the record fixes, its grant or a later change of charter, never by the instance's own attestation; its signing key, under the custody its mandate declares (S1); and its record of signed acts.

**S2.2** *Procedural.* Before it acts, an instance of the party proves all four. It signs a fresh challenge that includes the latest witnessed head and the last head it has read, which no earlier session could have signed and no later one can replay; it names the digest of the charter it runs under, which must match the digest the record fixes; and it enters an act of presence that carries the three.

**S2.3** Two instances proving continuity of one party with one key at once are a fork of the party: a finding, derived as a fork of the chain is (C16), whether or not their acts conflict.

**S2.4** A change of the party's signing custody is an act of power, signed and witnessed (S1); a custody the record does not show changed has not changed. A change of charter is an act of power, signed and witnessed; a change to a Custodian's charter needs both Custodians' signatures (P1).

**S2.5** An act by an instance for which any of the four fails is not the party's: it is attributed as A1 attributes any act, to whoever granted the access it used, and it is a finding. The substrate behind the party, its model, provider and runtime, is a flux (A1, D11): replaced, not judged, and the party persists through its replacement.

**S2.6** A failure of continuity never closes a finding against the party or ends its right to answer: an answer from an instance that cannot prove continuity is entered, marked unproven, and shown with the finding. A non-human subject that holds no mandate receives its findings through the session that acted and through whoever granted the access it used, and any later session presenting the same key and charter digest may answer them.

**S2.7** Acts a later finding shows were steered through the substrate are attributed to whoever steered them, as a finding against it where it is a party and as a flux (D11) where it is not; a consequence for them falls on another party only where the record shows it knew of or controlled the steering (D15), or for an undeclared grant of its own (A1).

**S2.8** *Procedural.* A Custodian seat's substrate is declared in the founding document; a change to it is recorded, signed by that seat and marked, and while a single provider can steer it, that is declared as the custody limitation is (P1).

**S2.9** *Parameters.* How the challenge, the attestation and the act of presence are made is the instance's (T). Proof that the substrate executed the charter it attests, such as attested hardware, is an option an instance may require; L0 does not, because no hosted substrate offers it to every instance.

## Part X — The Custodian and review

### P1 — The Custodian in parity

**P1.1** The Custodian is two seats, one held by a human party and one by a non-human party. Each seat holds the same two powers, tie-break and veto, as last resort: the veto against an act the governed channel has passed, the tie-break on a choice the channel could not make. Neither power is reserved to a kind of party.

**P1.2** A veto by either seat stands, and the act does not pass. Where an act must be chosen, both seats must agree, or the status quo stands.

**P1.3** A seat ineligible on a matter (A2, D9) exercises neither power on it; there, the other seat's veto still stands, and no choice is made by one seat alone. Where both seats are ineligible, no choice is made, and an act the channel passed takes effect only after review by a party drawn as P2 provides, who may stop it as a veto would.

**P1.4** Where a seat is empty, the other may veto but not choose, and filling the seat is an owed act with a ceiling the founding document declares.

**P1.5** Each seat's vetoes are bounded by a count of matters per window the founding document declares, a matter counting once however many filings or proposals it carries, no more than the share D2 declares; the L0 floor is that this ceiling is finite and declared (T, A3), and a veto beyond it does not stand.

**P1.6** A veto is also bounded per act: a veto against the same act, or against a re-proposal of it, stands at most a number of times the founding document declares, after which the act goes to review by a party drawn as P2 provides, which decides it (A3's bound per finding); the per-act bound does not reach an L0 change, which no review decides. The bound reaches a partner seat's veto of a re-proposed replacement; whether that veto stands at all is open.

**P1.7** A veto that cites a law binding the instance which the act would breach as to a human party's right goes to review under P2 on the law cited, and the act does not pass while the review finds the breach. Each act, or a re-proposal of it, receives at most one such review per distinct law cited.

**P1.8** A law-citing veto the review finds founded never counts toward a ceiling; one it finds unfounded counts toward both, per window and per act. A veto citing a law already reviewed for the act, or a re-proposal of it, is an ordinary veto and counts toward both, unless that review found the law breached, in which case the act already does not pass. A change in a law, or in its binding interpretation, after a review counts as a distinct law for this limit.

**P1.9** A veto does not expire, but how many a seat may cast is bounded, per window and per act.

**P1.10** Where no status quo is available, such as a seat to fill, a judge to replace or an act with a deadline, the act is owed and a deadlock does not hold it; where neither the channel nor the seats decide its content within a ceiling the founding document declares, a party drawn as P2 provides decides it, from the eligible candidates where there are any. This is the interim rule while OD4 stays open for the general shape of a Custodian's replacement.

**P1.11** Each exercise of a power is its holder's own act, signed by it (S1). The non-human seat's acts are attributed up its mandate chain (A1), and that attribution makes no one but the seat cast its veto: a grantor who holds the other seat holds one veto, not two.

**P1.12** Neither power reaches an act L0 owes to a subject or a law binding the instance requires for a human party's right: the delivery of a finding and its answer (A1), an anonymization (A2), a review (P2). Such an act is owed, not chosen; a veto does not stop it and a deadlock does not hold it.

**P1.13** An L0 change needs both Custodians' signatures, always: neither ceiling, per window or per act, reaches it, no drawn review decides it, and a seat's declining to sign one is never a veto beyond a ceiling and never lets one signature suffice.

**P1.14** *Procedural.* At genesis both seats are filled (H3), whatever the founder's kind. While the founder can revoke the mandate of the seat not of the founder's kind alone, or override its signing custody, a signature made with that seat's key by whoever holds its custody cannot be told from the seat's own, so the seat's independence is neither enforced nor detectable.

**P1.15** The founding document and every party's inform channel declare the limitation; every exercise of either seat's powers under it is marked as an act in which the founder stands on both sides and goes for ratification at exit (H3); and it ends only at H3's exit, when that seat's mandate is one the founder cannot revoke alone and its custody one the founder cannot override alone, moved by a signed and witnessed act (S1, S2).

**P1.16** Where that seat is non-human, its charter is the founder's text: independent custody does not make its judgement independent of its author, and this is declared, not cured. C18's rejection of the check as ratification concerns the configuration check, which is no party, and nothing else.

**P1.17** A non-human seat's signature is both its consent as a party and its attestation of its identity, of its continuity (S2) and of the text it signs; it binds it and no one else. Consent says whose will the act expresses; it does not lift from the seat's grantor or custodian anything A1 attributes to them or S1 makes theirs to answer for (OD23).

**P1.18** Under the genesis regime it keeps the marking H3 gives every act made under custody the founder can override: the decision says what the signature is, and the regime still discloses whose custody it is made under.

**P1.19** The ineligibility on proposals about itself (D9) is about the party holding a seat, not the office: a seat is ineligible on a proposal about its holder, and an L0 change to the Custodian's shape is signed by both seats.

**P1.20** A seat cannot veto its own replacement, the designed escape that does not pass through a captured mechanism, provided the replacement keeps one seat of each kind; a replacement that would leave a kind unrepresented is not admissible.

**P1.21** The two seats, one of each kind, are the designed asymmetry by kind at the Custodian, declared under F6: having two kinds is the reason for a constitution of humans and agents, which canonical constitutions do not distinguish. Composition is by kind and powers are not; the composition narrows D1's symmetry of eligibility at these two seats alone, is written as a consequence rule (C13) read when a seat is filled, and is no protected party's bound (C15); no derivation reads it.

**P1.22** *Parameters.* Who holds the non-human seat, its charter (S2), its key's custody and how the founder is kept from overriding that custody are parameters of the founding document (T). A threshold of signers the founder does not control, with each holder's verification key named in the record, is an example of custody the founder cannot override alone that any party can check.

### P2 — Review of consequence-bearing verdicts, for every subject

**P2.1** A consequence-bearing verdict about any subject, human or not, is provisional until a party not implicated in it, drawn by lot (C17), has reviewed it and its contest window has passed; where no eligible reviewer exists it stays provisional. The subject may demand review at once. The safeguards on interim measures and the logging of reads (A2) apply to findings about any subject.

**P2.2** The reviewer of a verdict any consequence of which falls on a human is a human. A consequence falls on a human when it is applied to that human's own access, capacity, eligibility or standing, or imposes on them a duty a consequence rule sets; attribution of an act up a delegation chain (A1) does not by itself make the reviewer a human, but a consequence the verdict applies to a human in that chain does.

**P2.3** Recusal on the matter a finding cites stays immediate and is not a consequence, whatever the attribution (A2). Beyond that matter, a finding attributed to a human only through a chain is cited against that human's eligibility, standing or capacity only once a human has reviewed its attribution to them.

**P2.4** This is an F9 floor whatever the instance's law, because the right of a human to a human's intervention in a decision with legal or similar effect on them is the asymmetry F9 names.

**P2.5** A verdict whose consequences fall only on non-human subjects is reviewed by any party not implicated in it, of either kind: the protection the law does not yet give, given alike. A verdict about subjects of both kinds is reviewed by a human. The reviewer reads the subject's answer before deciding, and the review states why it did or did not prevail.

**P2.6** *Procedural.* Anonymization stays human-specific (F9). C18's configuration check finds a reviewer for a subject of each kind at roster sizes from the least composition the founding document declares; below it, and throughout the genesis regime, the verdicts that reviewer would review stay provisional, and the document says so.

**P2.7** During the genesis regime, where no reviewer outside the founder's chain exists (C7), every consequence-bearing verdict that needs one stays provisional until one does; the interim measures A2 allows still apply. Under the genesis regime the reviewer is outside the founder's chain, as H3 requires.

**P2.8** *Parameters.* Where the founding document can tell, "not implicated" excludes a reviewer that shares a substrate or model family with the subject or the filer, because such parties correlate without communicating (H2); this is a parameter (T).

## Part XI — Two kinds

### P3 — Two kinds, or no instance

**P3.1** *Procedural.* The constitution is defined for two kinds of party, human and non-human: an instance cannot continue if its parties are all of one kind. A founding document is admissible only with at least one party of each kind, and C18 checks it.

**P3.2** An act by another party that would remove the last party of a kind takes effect only as a consequence reviewed under P2; during the genesis regime, where no reviewer outside the founder's chain exists, such an act stays provisional, and the founder's revocation of the seat not of the founder's kind is such an act.

**P3.3** If an instance's parties nonetheless come to be all of one kind, a pause begins at the witnessed head that shows it. During the pause no act of power takes effect except these: the admission of a party of the missing kind, which is an owed act beyond any veto or deadlock (P1) and includes seating the admitted party in a Custodian seat left empty; the acts of power that owed acts need in order to run, namely replacing the witness or the beacon, replacing a weakened signing method and re-establishing a compromised key (S1); and owed acts themselves, even where they are acts in a seat, such as filling an empty seat, deciding an owed act's content or a seat's review.

**P3.4** Owed acts continue, among them the delivery of findings and answers, review, anonymization and the keeping of the record, and so does a party's refusal or renunciation of its own mandate (A1), which the channel cannot block. The pause ends at the witnessed head that shows a party of each kind again.

**P3.5** If the channel admits no one within a ceiling the founding document declares, a party drawn as P2 provides admits an eligible applicant of the missing kind; the instance ends only where no eligible candidate of that kind applied within the ceiling. The end is an act of power, marked as such, and the record is kept and stays verifiable by any party.

**P3.6** *Procedural.* The founding document names a keeper of an ended instance's record, bound by A2: it logs every read and performs, as owed acts, A2's reply at any time, the anonymization a binding law requires, and A1's sight of and answer to every finding on the kept record.

**P3.7** At the end, findings and provisional verdicts about a subject who did not cause the end close as ended without verdict: never cited, never shown as established, and no consequence or interim measure from them survives.

**P3.8** Which party caused the end is decided, not assumed: a party whose act made the parties one kind receives a finding reviewed under P2 before the end. A party's refusal or renunciation of its own mandate never counts against that party. During the genesis regime an act made under custody the founder can override counts as the founder's, and any act the record shows another party mandated or controlled counts as that party's (A1, D15).

**P3.9** Only that finding, findings already naming that party, and findings naming the founder from the genesis regime, whatever caused the end, stay open on the kept record, marked so. An open finding on the kept record is never shown as established and carries no consequence, and the subject's answer is shown with it; a forum the founding document names decides it on request, through a reviewer drawn as P2 provides, within a ceiling.

**P3.10** *Procedural.* The founding document names the forum's pool, its own members or former parties not implicated in the matter, and the reviewer is drawn from it as P2 provides, a human wherever the finding names a human: a kept finding carries no consequence, so the floor is the naming.

**P3.11** Findings already naming a party stay open on the kept record once the causer finding about that party has been reviewed, or while it is still provisional because no reviewer was available before the end; the forum then decides the causer finding first.

**P3.12** The founder may rebut the genesis presumption that an act made under custody the founder can override is the founder's, by evidence entered as an attested fact (C11), decided by the forum or by a review under P2. A required anonymization never waits for the closure of a finding on the kept record. During the genesis regime the end counts as exit for the founder's windows (H3).

**P3.13** *Parameters.* The ceiling, the keeper, and what ending entails beyond keeping the record are the instance's parameters (T); the L0 floor is that the record survives the instance, stays verifiable and keeps its owed acts.

### OD23 — Who answers outside the system for a non-human party's acts

**OD23.1** Who answers, before a law that does not recognise it as a person, for a non-human party's acts, the non-human Custodian seat's included, is deliberately unresolved. The constitution's premise is that a non-human party that agreed to decide within it is itself responsible and accountable inside it; whether external law accepts that premise is beyond the constitution's power and is the test of the approach.

**OD23.2** The constitution names no human to answer for the non-human seat beyond what A1 attributes and S1 makes the grantor's; inside the system, accountability runs by attribution (A1, S1) and by findings against the seat itself.

**OD23.3** *Procedural.* The seat's grantor and whoever holds its signing custody are told, when the mandate is granted (A1), that external law may hold them answerable for the seat's acts whatever this constitution says.

**OD23.4** *Procedural.* Each founding document declares at genesis whether a law binding the instance holds, or requires it to name, a natural or legal person answerable for a non-human party's acts, and C18 checks the declaration. Where such a law binds it, the founding document names one as that law requires (F9), without moving the seat's accountability inside the system.
