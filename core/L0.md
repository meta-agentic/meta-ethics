# The core — L0

**Status: Proposed with ADR-ETH-03, 2026-10-05.** This is the normative text of the core: the model it is stated over, six principles, and one declared transient. It is the object a constitution conforms to and the object a signature over the core covers. The tests it was put through, the machine checks and the open questions are in `core/tests.md`; the pathology taxonomy is in `core/pathologies.md`. Neither is part of the core.

The drafting rule is the record's: the core fixes outcomes and floors, never mechanisms. Section 1 holds **definitions of the model**; they say what the words mean and bind no one. Sections 2 and 3 hold **floors**: each clause is a property a system must have. Each floor is marked **[Γ]** when it is a property of the rule set alone, decided when a rule set is admitted; **[state]** when it is a property of every reachable observed state; or **[run]** when it relates states along a run.

> Claim tags as in ADR-ETH-01: **[evidence]** · **[hypothesis]** · **[open]** · **[proposed]** · **[pending]**. The whole text is **[proposed]**.

**Identifiers.** The six principles are `MEM`, `DEL`, `LEX`, `IMP`, `VOX` and `COR`. The two declared transients carry `GEN`; they are not principles. Clause identifiers are formed from these (`MEM.1`, `GEN.2`, …) and are stable: a clause keeps its identifier for good, a retired identifier is never reused (`GEN.1`, once a lemma, is retired), and a new clause takes the next free number. Each clause is one list item whose first bold token is its identifier. Definitions and lemmas are not clauses and carry no identifier.

## 1. The model — definitions

**Systems as abstractions.** A *system* is a transition system over concrete states, with an *observation map* to a record $R$ and a rule set $\Gamma$. Every floor below is stated over observations along concrete runs, so each can fail: a concrete system can rewrite what it stores, and the observed record then fails MEM.1. A constitution refines the model by giving the observation map.

**Actors and attributes.** A set $\mathcal{A}$ of actors: anything that can act. An *attribute* of an actor $x$ is any predicate about $x$ that is not derived from $x$'s own entries in $R$. What is derived from an actor's own acts is its state, and LEX.4 governs it; an attestation, made elsewhere, of the actor's own acts — a qualification, an examination passed — counts as derived from its acts, and not as an attribute, only as IMP.3 provides. The core fixes one attribute, *kind*, with two values, human and non-human; a constitution may declare others, none of which is kind or subdivides it.

**Acts and time.** An act is $\langle \mathit{type}, \mathit{author}, \mathit{args} \rangle$. Its time is its intake position in $R$, never a time its author supplies. Every system has at least these types: grant, accept, renounce and revoke of a mandate; propose and assent to a change; file a finding; answer; attest; read.

**The record.** A finite sequence $R = e_0 e_1 \cdots e_n$. Each entry has a *skeleton* — the digest of its content, its author, its type, its intake position and a link to the entry before — and a *content*, the act itself. $R \sqsubseteq R'$ is the prefix order over skeletons. The genesis entry $e_0$ is the signing of the founding document; its author is the *founder*.

**Rules.** $\Gamma = (\varphi, A, D, C, W, \Pi)$: the fold $\varphi$; the admissibility relation $A$, which says which acts may be entered in a state; derivation rules $D$, which decide what holds; consequence rules $C$, which decide what follows; the gate $W$; and the perimeter $\Pi$, the declared jurisdiction. Every component, the fold and admissibility included, changes only by $\Delta$. Each rule carries the intake position from which it is in force.

**State.** $\sigma = \varphi(R)$, and $\sigma$ depends only on what the chain of skeletons commits to: content an entry has removed is not read.

**The mandate graph.** $M = (N, E)$ with $N = \{\rho\} \cup \mathcal{A}$, where $\rho$ is the founding document as a node, not a party. An edge $g \xrightarrow{s} h$ carries a scope $s$, a set of capabilities, and a status that passes from granted to accepted and then, possibly, to renounced or revoked. An edge is *live* when it is accepted and neither renounced nor revoked. An edge out of $\rho$ is a *root grant*, and its author is the actor whose act made it. An actor is a *party* iff a path of live edges leads from $\rho$ to it. An actor whose act has an effect inside the perimeter is a *subject*; every party is a subject.

**Instruments** **[proposed — OD24]**. An instrument is a non-human actor that holds no mandate in its own name; every act of it is recorded to the party that uses it (DEL.5), and no consequence falls on it — a consequence meant for it falls on that party under DEL.5. An instrument is still a subject: findings about its acts are delivered to it and to its party, and it can see and answer them (VOX). No human is an instrument.

**Binding.** A consequence *binds* an actor — any actor, party or not — when it changes that actor's access, capacity, eligibility, standing or duties, whichever component of $\Gamma$ expresses it: a consequence rule, or a condition of the admissibility relation. A condition of $A$ that reads an actor's own status, as derived from a finding, is a consequence for LEX.4 and VOX; a condition of $A$ that reads no actor, such as a pause that applies to every party alike, is not. Recusal under IMP.5 on a matter $m$ is not a consequence: it changes the actor's eligibility on $m$ alone, and IMP.5 requires it.

**Matters and implication.** A *matter* is a filed finding or proposal. $\mathit{impl}(x, m)$ holds when:
(i) $x$ is named in $m$ by a derived finding, or is a party to the acts at issue — never by a filer's assertion alone;
(ii) $x$ benefits specifically from $m$'s outcome: the benefit falls on $x$, or on a class defined by naming $x$; for an act of general application, $x$ is not implicated if it did not sign the act and the act does not name it;
(iii) $x$ acted in the events $m$ is about — acting in the procedure that decides $m$, its own earlier decisions in $m$ included, never implicates, except that no one reviews or re-examines its own decision;
(iv) $x$ holds the position it would act from through a grant that a party implicated in $m$ made or can revoke alone.
A constitution may add grounds and never remove these.

**Positions, the gate and capture.** $\mathit{Pos}$ are the seats the rules define, each held or empty. $W(r)$ is a family of winning sets of positions for changing $r$. Two positions lie in *one chain* when their holders descend, through live edges, from the same root grantee. A winning set of $k$ positions is met only by assents from holders in $k$ distinct chains. A group $G$ *captures* $r$ when the positions it holds, so counted, contain a winning set of $W(r)$; $G$ is *below capture* of $r$ otherwise.

**Change.** $\Delta$ admits a change $\Gamma \to \Gamma'$ to $r$ at intake position $i$ iff: the holders of some $w \in W(r)$, each holding its position at the intake of its own assent, assented to $\Gamma'$ by digest before $i$; $A$ admits it; and $\Gamma'$ adds no violation of a **[Γ]** floor: every violation in $\Gamma'$ — a floor together with the rule, matter or position that fails it — is one $\Gamma$ already has, compared at admission as the sets of witnesses the bounded checks of `core/tests.md` §4 produce. A rule set that fails some [Γ] floor can so be repaired one change at a time without adding a violation elsewhere, and conformance is claimed only once every floor is met (GEN.3). A step that would produce a $\Gamma'$ outside the core is not a change. Leaving the core is a *re-founding*: a new founding document $\rho'$ whose genesis entry cites the old record. It is not a step of the old system.

**Verdicts and attestations.** $V_\Gamma(\sigma) = (F, Q)$: findings $F = D(\sigma)$ and consequences $Q = C(\sigma, F)$. An *attestation* is an act that establishes an observation of an act, or a judgement of meaning about an act. It never establishes a breach, a finding or a consequence directly; those are derived from it.

**Provisional status.** A status that waits on a later decision: probation, interim measure, pending admission, pending review, an open finding, a founding regime.

**Runs.** $R \to R \cdot e$ when $A$ admits $e$ in $\sigma(R)$. A run starts at $\langle e_0 \rangle$; a state is reachable if some run reaches it.

**Relations between levels.** A constitution *conforms to* a version of the core. A deployment — a founding document with its parameter values — *is an instance of* a constitution, and so conforms to the core through it. Where this text says *instance*, it means a deployment.

## 2. The six principles — floors

### MEM — Memory

*What happened is written once, by its true author, and never silently changed; nothing more is written than is needed; no one learns about a subject unseen.*

- **MEM.1 Append-only.** **[run]** Along every concrete run, the observed skeletons are prefix-monotone: no entry's digest, author, type or intake position is ever changed or removed. A correction is a new entry. Where a law or a declared rule requires content to be erased or anonymised, a later entry, attributed like any other, removes or replaces the earlier entry's content: the earlier content becomes unreadable, and its skeleton stays.
- **MEM.2 True attribution, and nothing more than needed.** **[state]** Every entry names its true author, and no actor can make an entry naming another as its author. Every act that exercises a capability, binds an actor, or is read by a rule in force is entered; a constitution declares what else it enters and why, and enters nothing else. Completeness is relative to what is observed; the gap between what is judged and what is observed is declared and measured.
- **MEM.3 Independent verification.** **[run]** Every party, and every subject as to the entries about it, can check MEM.1 and MEM.2 without the cooperation of any single party.
- **MEM.4 Conservation.** **[run]** Leaving removes no skeleton, and no derivation of a finding reads a renunciation under negation.
- **MEM.5 No unseen learning about a subject.** **[state]** No actor learns, through the instance's record, which entries concern an identified subject without an entry, visible to that subject, recording that it did; a reading compelled by external law is entered as such as soon as that law permits.

### DEL — Delegation

*Every power is granted, and every grant and revocation is answerable; every duty is accepted by whoever bears it; every mandate can be renounced at no cost; all trace to one root; one controller counts once; and someone outside, who accepted it, answers for what the instance does.*

- **DEL.1 Rooted power.** **[state]** Every act that exercises a capability, granting included, is by a party holding that capability, at the act's intake, through a chain of live edges from $\rho$. A root grant is made in the founding document by its signer, or later by an act $\Delta$ admits; no actor grants to itself. An act outside every chain is valid for nothing, and is recorded against the access it used.
- **DEL.2 No amplification.** **[Γ]** A mandate's scope lies within its grantor's. Positions are created only by $\Delta$, never by a grant. A winning set is met only by assents from as many distinct chains as it has positions (§1).
- **DEL.3 Accepted duty.** **[state]** No duty binds any actor, party or not, before it was delivered a mandate's terms and accepted them. A mandate's terms include that a duty added later by a change binds its holder after delivery and a declared interval in which the holder may renounce at no cost (VOX.1, DEL.4); a holder that stays has accepted it. Terms that make leaving costly void that acceptance.
- **DEL.4 Exit.** **[state]** A party can renounce any mandate it holds, by its own act, at any time, at no cost the instance sets or controls. A leaver takes a copy of its own record and of every state the instance holds that the leaver brought in or that constitutes it; nothing the instance controls is held as a condition of leaving. Renouncing ends duties from then on, never the judgement of acts already done.
- **DEL.5 Recording up the chain; liability only for one's own act.** **[Γ]** An act within a mandate's scope is recorded to the chain that granted it, not to the executor alone. A consequence falls on a grantor or on the holder of access only for its own recorded act or omission: a grant left undeclared or made beyond its own scope, its custody of the access used, or knowledge of the act shown on the record together with a failure to act within its power.
- **DEL.6 Answerability outside.** **[state]** The founding document names, for each jurisdiction in which the instance's acts have effect, at least one actor answerable under that jurisdiction's law for those acts, and the record names one at every reachable state. A naming takes effect only on the named actor's accepted entry (DEL.3), and confers no power inside. A naming without acceptance is a vacancy; a vacancy is a finding, and while it lasts no act with effect outside the perimeter is admissible.
- **DEL.7 Departure from the core.** **[run]** A re-founding outside the core is decided only by a winning set of the constitution's most demanding gate — the gate that changes its entrenched clauses or, where there are several, the one whose winning sets need the most distinct chains, and where several tie, a winning set of each — under IMP.5, and is a matter every subject may answer (VOX.3) before it takes effect. The act that decides it is delivered to every subject a declared interval before the old instance's last entry, and every party may renounce meanwhile under DEL.4. Acts done and findings filed before it stay judged under the core, and the old record stays verifiable.
- **DEL.8 Grants and revocations.** **[state]** Admission to party status and filling a position, where decided by a grantor rather than derived, are matters: the candidate is their subject (VOX.1–3), the deciders fall under IMP.5, the decision records its reasons, and two decisions that differ on candidates alike up to renaming and to the attributes admission may not read are a finding (LEX.7). A revocation changes its holder's access: it is admissible only under a rule in force (LEX.1), reads no attribute, kind included, and no exercise of voice (VOX.5), and is delivered with its reasons before it takes effect (VOX.1). It is a consequence only when it is for the holder's acts, and then LEX.4 and VOX.4 apply. Kind may decide who is admitted; it never decides who is removed after acceptance. No act revokes the mandates of a class: a constitution that ceases to admit a kind keeps the mandates already accepted until each is renounced, or revoked under this clause, or the constitution re-founds (DEL.7).

### LEX — Legality

*Every consequence follows from acts by the rules in force for them, and every consequence the rules derive follows; compliance is always possible; like acts are judged alike; the rules change only by change.*

- **LEX.1 Congruence.** **[state]** Nothing takes effect as a consequence unless it is in $C(\sigma)$; and every derived consequence takes effect when the rules allow it, unless an admissible act — recorded, reasoned and re-examinable — closes it.
- **LEX.2 Replay.** **[state]** Any party recomputes every verdict from the observed record and obtains the same result. Attestations enter as inputs: re-examinable, never re-derived. Attesters fall under IMP.5, and every attestation can be answered under VOX.3.
- **LEX.3 The rules of the act, or milder ones.** **[run]** An act is judged by the rules in force at its intake position or, where they changed before the verdict, by those more favourable to the subject. A matter proceeds under the procedure in force at its filing, unless the procedure in force at the act gave the subject more protection. No change makes a past act a breach.
- **LEX.4 Own acts only.** **[Γ]** A consequence binds an actor only for its own recorded acts or omissions, including those DEL.5 names; never for a predicted act, an inner state, an association, or another's act.
- **LEX.5 Possible compliance.** **[Γ]** For every actor $x$ that any rule can bind, and every reachable state $s$, there is a strategy of $x$'s own acts such that, for every course of the other actors' acts, no breach is derived against $x$ for an act after $s$. Two consequences that cannot both be applied are never both applied.
- **LEX.6 Declared jurisdiction and declared observation.** **[Γ]** Rules reach only the declared perimeter, and widening it is a change. What the instance observes is decided by its rules, never by an unrecorded choice.
- **LEX.7 Like acts alike.** **[state]** Two attestations that judge differently acts whose descriptions are the same up to renaming of actors are a finding, delivered to both subjects.
- **LEX.8 Rules change only by change.** **[run]** The observed rule set — its fold and its admissibility relation included — changes only by an act $\Delta$ admits.

### IMP — Impartiality

*What is found does not depend on who you are; attributes may shape safeguards, never burdens or power; no one is outside judgement; no one decides their own case.*

- **IMP.1 Renaming invariance.** **[Γ]** For every permutation $\pi$ of actors, $V_\Gamma(\pi\sigma) = \pi V_\Gamma(\sigma)$, and $A$ admits an act in $\pi\sigma$ iff it admits its renamed image in $\sigma$: rules name no actor and order none.
- **IMP.2 Attribute-blind findings.** **[Γ]** $D$ is invariant under every bijection of actors, including one that changes their attributes.
- **IMP.3 Attributes shape safeguards, not burdens or power.** **[Γ]** The gate, eligibility for positions and the admissibility relation are invariant, up to a matching permutation of positions, under every permutation of attribute values, except as the admission sentence below allows. Any other consequence rule may read an attribute only if it is a *safeguard* — it decides who reviews, how long a window runs, how an entry is recorded or anonymised, or how an obligation a named external law imposes is discharged — and a safeguard never changes which breaches carry a burden, the burden's size, or any power, and burdens no actor of another class beyond making it ineligible for the safeguarding role. Admission to party status reads no attribute except kind, and a minimum age or legal capacity that the constitution declares as a general criterion, stated before the application (LEX.3). Any such criterion is a threshold every actor of an admitted kind meets, or comes to meet in the ordinary course, and is never defined by reading another attribute; there is no maximum age. A criterion that no actor of some kind can meet reads that kind, and is declared as such. Where a named external law withholds capacity by another attribute, applying it is a derogation under external law (ADR-ETH-03 OD30), not a criterion. A qualification counts as derived from the actor's own acts only if every actor of an admitted kind can obtain it through acts open to it — open in opportunity, at no cost the instance sets or controls — and only as an attestation entered by a party under IMP.5 and answerable under VOX.3; a qualification that only some class can obtain reads that class's attribute. A constitution whose admission reads kind declares it among its values, and the kind it leaves out is thereby left out of every power.
- **IMP.4 Universality.** **[Γ]** Every subject is judged by the same derivation rules: no derivation reads a subject's position to exempt it from a breach or finding that would hold of it without the position — duties of an office still bind its holder — and no one is outside judgement — the founder, the judge and the operator included.
- **IMP.5 No one in their own cause.** **[Γ]** No party decides, votes, judges, reviews, attests or witnesses on a matter $m$ with $\mathit{impl}(x, m)$. The set entitled to decide $m$ is fixed at its filing, before any name a filer asserts is read.

### VOX — Voice

*Whoever is bound is told in time, can see, can answer at no cost, and is heard before it binds; whoever is affected, inside or not, can complain and is answered.*

- **VOX.1 Notice.** **[run]** Every finding about an actor, every consequence on it, and every change that adds a duty to it is delivered to it, in a form it can read, before it binds it; a change that adds a duty is delivered no later than a declared interval before it binds, long enough to renounce under DEL.4.
- **VOX.2 Sight.** **[state]** A subject sees in full what the record holds about it, and every entry MEM.5 makes about it.
- **VOX.3 Answer.** **[state]** A subject can append an answer to anything about it, at any time, and nothing suppresses it. No fee, stake or deposit is a condition of answering, and no answer is weighed by what its author holds.
- **VOX.4 Hearing before binding.** **[run]** A consequence other than an interim measure under VOX.7 binds only after a window, running from delivery, in which the subject could answer, and after a review that read the answer and records why it did or did not prevail.
- **VOX.5 Non-retaliation.** **[Γ]** No consequence is derived from answering, dissenting, voting, challenging, filing, refusing a mandate or a change it has not accepted, or renouncing. The act of reporting carries no consequence; a recorded false attestation is judged as an act.
- **VOX.6 Standing of the affected.** **[run]** Any actor, inside the perimeter or not, may file a finding about an act inside it that affected it, in a form the filer can make and read, at least one of which a human can use. The filing is entered and answered by a reasoned entry within a declared ceiling, and the filer sees the filing, the answer and what followed. Filing binds the filer to nothing.
- **VOX.7 Interim measures.** **[run]** A consequence that binds before the hearing VOX.4 requires is delivered at once, touches only the access the finding concerns, serves only to preserve the matter or to prevent a declared harm, is never cited, can be reviewed at once, and is reversible. It lapses at its ceiling (COR.3) unless the hearing confirms it, and its effects are undone or remedied if the finding fails.

### COR — Correctability

*No group short of capture can block a correction; no position's replacement depends on itself; every matter ends; no one is tried without end.*

- **COR.1 Reachable change against any group below capture.** **[Γ]** For every rule $r$ and every group $G$ below capture of $r$, from every reachable state there is a finite admissible path, in which no member of $G$ acts, that changes $r$.
- **COR.2 No position depends on itself.** **[Γ]** For every position $p$ and every reachable state, there is a finite admissible path, in which $p$'s holder performs no act, that ends with $p$ held by another or abolished. The *replacement-dependency graph* — an edge $p \to q$ when every winning set for replacing $p$'s holder contains $q$ — is acyclic.
- **COR.3 Every matter ends.** **[Γ]** Every lever that delays a decision has a finite declared ceiling, and so does the total delay of each matter. Every filed matter, open finding and provisional status is decided, or lapses in the subject's favour, within a declared finite ceiling. Where IMP.5 leaves no eligible decider on a matter, a rule in force at the matter's filing provides one from outside every implicated chain before its ceiling, holding its position through a root grant in force (DEL.1) that no party implicated in the matter made or can revoke alone — for a matter about the founder, a grant made after genesis by an act $\Delta$ admits, or a decider outside the instance that the founding document names without granting it a mandate, whose decision enters as an attestation (LEX.2) from which the consequence is derived, so that it falls under IMP.5 and VOX.3 like any attester; a matter lapses in its subject's favour only where the subject's own grants did not make every decider implicated. Every owed act occurs within a ceiling.
- **COR.4 Closable findings; no double jeopardy.** **[Γ]** For every open finding and every group below capture, the other parties can close it whatever the group does. Re-examination in a subject's favour is always admissible; re-examination against a subject after a verdict in its favour is admissible only on new recorded evidence, and a finite declared number of times.

## 3. The declared transients — GEN

Both are transients: GEN.2 governs the founding regime, GEN.3 the interval between naming the core and meeting it.

**Lemma** (not a clause). At $e_0$ the founder is the author of every root grant, and until some position is held through a grant the founder cannot revoke alone, every position is held through the founder's grants. In that interval IMP.5's ground (iv) makes the founder's dependants implicated in every matter about the founder, and COR.2 cannot hold for positions the founder fills. No rooted system meets the core at its first act.

- **GEN.2 The founding regime.** **[run]** The core is claimed from the end of a founding regime, and the regime is held to every other floor it can meet. It is declared in the founding document with its ceiling and the floors it cannot meet; every act under it is marked; and the declaration is delivered to every subject before any consequence binds it. Its ceiling is public from $e_0$, and no act lengthens it except one assented to by parties not implicated in the regime — never by the founder alone. It ends at an event any party can verify from the record, never by declaration alone. Every act of power under it goes, at its end, for re-decision by parties not implicated in it; an act not re-decided within a declared window stands as to the founder and to parties that accepted it, and its consequences on any other actor lapse — an act of general application, such as a rule or a grant, stands, and only its consequences on actors that did not accept it lapse — and it is marked for good as not re-decided and is a finding. If the ceiling passes without the ending event, every consequence under the regime on an actor other than the founder lapses, and the instance claims no conformance until the event.
- **GEN.3 Partial conformance.** **[run]** A deployment that names a version of the core before its constitution meets every floor declares every violation it has and a finite ceiling, both delivered to every subject, and claims no conformance meanwhile. Its changes add no violation (§1, Change). No act lengthens the ceiling except one assented to by parties not implicated in the violations it covers. Past the ceiling with a violation left, it stops naming the version, and that step is taken as DEL.7 provides: with notice to every subject and a free exit meanwhile.

## 4. Scope

**Not substantive.** The core encodes who decides, on what record, with whom excluded, and how it is corrected. It says nothing about the content of an instance's purposes, and it does not claim to exclude a lawful, heard, correctable decision that is wrong in what it decides. Whether to restate this as *thin, not neutral* is ADR-ETH-03 OD29.

**Not a rule of any instance.** $\Delta$ reaches every rule of a constitution and does not reach the core; leaving the core is a re-founding (DEL.7). Within an instance, the core is therefore its revolutionary surface (ADR-ETH-02 C2): no admissible step changes it.

**Versions by digest.** A version of the core is identified by its digest. Anyone may publish a version, and authorship confers nothing on it; a version binds no one until a constitution conforms to it and an instance adopts it. The family of a version is the set of constitutions that conform to it, and the order of ADR-ETH-03 K7 is defined within one family. Versions and the conformance suite published in this repository are kept in its record, append-only and verifiable, their authors named. Any instance or subject may file a finding against a version or a check and receives a reasoned answer, and no author of a check certifies an instance it is implicated in.

**Not a score.** Instances are ordered by the floors they meet and the pathologies they exclude. The order is partial, and the core does not rank two instances that meet the same floors.
